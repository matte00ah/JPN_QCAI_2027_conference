# QCAI 2027 paper plan - hybrid quantum-kernel crater detection (proxy experiment)

Plan of the preliminary experiment for the paper submitted to the **2nd QCAI Workshop** (Quantum Computing - Artificial Intelligence for Industry Applications and Scientific Discovery), co-located with **IEEE QCNC 2027** (International Conference on Quantum Communications, Networking, and Computing), Hokkaido, Japan, 22-26 March 2027.

- Submission: EasyChair (`conf=qcnc2027`, select the workshop), IEEE conference two-column template, single-blind. Page limit to confirm with the organisers for PhD works (short paper 2-4 pages assumed; full paper is 6 pages, references included).
- Deadlines: **submission 2026-12-20**, notification 2027-01-20, camera-ready 2027-01-30.
- Effort budget: ~15 full working days (3-4 days/week from 2026-11-18), writing included.

**How to use this file** (same conventions as `preprocessing_plan.md`)
- Every atomic step has a checkbox and an id (e.g. `QC3.2`). Tick it only when the step is implemented *and* verified.
- Steps marked **[core]** are needed for the paper; steps marked **[opt]** are done only if time is left, in the order given in [section 2](#2---schedule-and-priorities); steps marked **[future]** ([section 20](#20---future-work)) are planned after the QCAI paper and are not part of its schedule.

- **Reproducibility of every statement.** Each number, check or observation written in this plan (and later in the README and in the paper) must come from a project script (`Experiments/qcai2027/`) run by a documented command, with its output saved in `Experiments/qcai2027/results/`: in the report of its phase (`qa_checks.json`, `qb_region_labels.json`, `qc_preprocessing.json`, `qd_data_analysis.json`, one section per step id), in the check figure of its phase (`figures/`) or in the paper material (`paper/`). ## 1 - Goal, scope and claims

### 1.1 - What the paper must show
A feasibility study of a **hybrid classical-quantum crater detector** on real lunar multimodal data (LROC WAC optical images: Wide Angle Camera of the Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter Camera; + SLDEM2015 elevation: the 2015 digital elevation model from LOLA, the Lunar Orbiter Laser Altimeter, and the Kaguya / SELENE stereo images), with:
1. a **multimodal fusion layer** (light cross-attention, trained self-supervised) that gives better representations than a single modality and than basic fusion (concatenation and similar);
2. a **quantum-kernel SVM** (QSVC, quantum support vector classifier) that re-classifies the candidate sub-tiles of a classical stage 1, run on a **statevector simulator**, compared on identical inputs with classical kernels;
3. an honest analysis of **when** the quantum kernel works or fails (kernel concentration, bandwidth, number of qubits, shot noise, hardware noise model) and of the **cost** of running it on real hardware.

No quantum advantage is claimed; the contribution is the architecture, the protocol and the first measurements.

**Status of claim 2 after QI4.4 (2026-11-23, QD11).** The QSVC filters the stage-1 candidates (end-to-end precision 0.35 against 0.166 of stage 1 at its threshold) but, **at the same recall, stage 1 alone with a stricter threshold is more precise** (0.57 against 0.35): on 16 principal components of one sub-tile and ≤ 2,000 samples, stage 2 ranks the candidates worse than the stage-1 score (AP, average precision, 0.44-0.45 against 0.60). The paper reports this as a result: the two-stage design, as built in this proxy, does not improve on stage 1; RQ2 (quantum vs classical kernels on the same compressed features) is unaffected and still answered by QJ. Ways to give stage 2 the information of stage 1 are future work ([section 20](#20---future-work), FW1).

### 1.2 - Research questions (each one answered by one table or figure, section 15)

| Id | Question | Answered by |
|---|---|---|
| RQ1 | Does the cross-attention fusion give better crater representations than WAC alone, DEM (digital elevation model) alone and basic fusion? | Table I (QG, QI6) |
| RQ2 | On the same compressed features, is a quantum kernel competitive with classical kernels for the stage-2 crater classification? | Fig. 2 (QI4, QJ) |
| RQ3 | Which encoding, number of qubits and bandwidth avoid kernel concentration, and is the quantum kernel geometrically different from the classical one? | Fig. 3 (QI5) |
| RQ4 | How much do shot noise and a realistic IBM noise model degrade the kernel and the classifier, and what would a run on hardware cost? | Table II (QK) |

### 1.3 - Scope
- **In**: Moon, one equatorial region (QD1), level L1 only (the finest level of the scale pyramid of the pipeline, 100 m/px), craters of 1-8 km, WAC 643 nm + SLDEM2015 (with DEM derivatives), frozen pre-trained backbones, light fusion layers trained on cached tokens, stage-1 classical head, QSVC on simulator, classical baselines, noise-model simulation of a small kernel.
- **Out**: icy/water anomaly branch (only described in the paper as future work), Mini-RF (LRO radar), LAMP (LRO ultraviolet spectrograph), WAC multispectral, polar regions, scale pyramid L2-L4, Mars transfer, fine-tuning of the backbones, real quantum hardware, re-training of end-to-end detectors (DeepMoon, YOLO-type detectors, You Only Look Once: only cited), the full `preprocessing_plan.md`.

### 1.4 - Differences from the main pipeline
| Pipeline (`proposed_pipeline_Claude_correction.md`) | Proxy experiment | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Up to K modalities, planetary-trained foundation models | 2 modalities, generic DINOv2 (self-supervised Vision Transformer of Meta AI) frozen (QD2) | time; the pipeline itself plans this as the "generic foundation model" baseline |
| Cross-attention fusion trained with several self-supervised objectives | Two light bidirectional cross-attention blocks, masked cross-modal token reconstruction + modality drop-out (QD4) | time; same family of objectives |
| Full pre-processing, common grids, pyramid | Native equirectangular grid of the region, L1 only (section 6) | time |
| QSVC + 3 QSVR (quantum-kernel support vector regressors) + NMS (non-maximum suppression), global catalogue | QSVC core; QSVR optional (QI7); no NMS (each sub-tile assigned once, QD8) | time |
| Validation on IBM hardware | Noise-model simulation of IBM devices (fake backends) | no hardware access |

    - **Denoising: none**, same principle as D3 (noise left to the models: no filter that could also remove small real features). The only smoothing operations are the block mean of QC2.1 (resampling) and the Gaussian of QC4.1 (used to remove the long wavelengths from the relief, not the noise).
    - **Photometric correction: none.** The WAC mosaic is used as delivered (D17): its topographic shading (62-83 % of the variance, B4.3) and its albedo are not separated; in R the Sun is from the west everywhere (D14, confirmed by the correlations of QC4.3). The normalisation of QC5.1 is one affine map with clipping per layer for the whole of R, not a photometric correction.
  - Evidence that the stored rasters carry no such correction: WAC windows identical to a direct reading of the products (QC1.1); DEM conversion error 0 m (QC1.2); DEM on the WAC grid against the native DEM: mean difference -0.008 m, standard deviation 3.4 m, i.e. only the block mean (QC2.1).
  - Paper (section 15, item 6), draft sentence: *"The inputs are used as delivered: no destriping, denoising or photometric correction is applied, so the WAC mosaic keeps its ~1.7 km cross-track stripes and its topographic shading, and the DEM is only averaged to the 100 m grid; this proxy pre-processing is a limitation of the study."*
- [ ] **QC6.2** [opt] Ablation: WAC with the comb notch filter of D12 (stripes at ~1.7 km have the size of one sub-tile and could create false periodic patterns in the tokens); compare the linear probe AP with and without it.

### QF1 - Variants compared (all produce one vector per sub-tile)

### QF2 - Cross-attention fusion F-X (needs QD4)
- [x] **QF2.1** [core] Input: the two 16 x 16 x 384 token maps. Linear projection to 256, plus a learned modality embedding and a 2-D sine-cosine positional embedding.
- [x] **QF2.2** [core] Two fusion blocks. In each block the WAC tokens attend to the DEM tokens and the DEM tokens attend to the WAC tokens (cross-attention, 4 heads), each followed by a self-attention over its own map and a feed-forward layer (pre-norm, residual).
  - Intuition: a WAC token looking at a dark crescent can "ask" the DEM tokens around it whether there is a depression and a rim; self-attention adds the neighbourhood (rims extend over several tokens).
- [x] **QF2.3** [core] Output per position: concatenation of the two updated tokens -> linear layer -> **512** (16 x 16 x 512 fused map, as in the pipeline).
  - QF2.1-QF2.3 done 2026-11-19 (`qc_fusion.py`: `build_fusion_x`, `sincos_2d`; hyper-parameters in the `fusion` section of `qc_config.yaml`; check `qc_fusion.py model` → `results/qf_fusion.json` section QF2, ~11 s on the login node). Choices taken with the user: QD4 taken with **2 blocks** (section 1.4 corrected, it said one block); **FFN ratio 4** (FFN = feed-forward network of the transformer block) (standard, 256 → 1,024 → 256), so F-X has **4.68 M parameters** instead of the ~3 M first estimated (projections 0.20 M, per block cross-attention 0.53 M, self-attention 0.53 M, FFN 1.05 M for the two streams, output 0.26 M); the learned **mask token** of each modality replaces the masked positions **after the projection**, before the modality and positional embeddings are added (MAE / MultiMAE style, i.e. masked autoencoders: a masked position still knows where it is and which modality is missing). Both cross-attentions of a block use the input state of the block (symmetric update); pre-norm residual layers; final LayerNorm per stream before the concatenation; Xavier initialisation of the linear layers, mask token and modality embedding N(0, 0.02).
  - Checks (untrained model, 8 random used tiles, CPU): output (8, 16, 16, 512), finite; forward and `encode` with attention weights equal within 2.2e-6 (different attention kernel); cross-attention weights (8 tiles, 4 heads, 256 x 256), rows sum to 1; changing the DEM tokens changes the WAC stream (mean change 0.14); with the WAC fully masked the output does not depend on the WAC values (mask token); a spatially shuffled input does not give the shuffled output (the position matters); every parameter gets a finite non-zero gradient with 50 % masks; forward of 128 tiles 0.95 s on 8 CPU threads, so the training of QF3 is light on a GPU.

Status: **proposed** = default of this plan, to confirm; **taken** = confirmed by the user.

  - Fusion trained with crater labels: easily beats the baselines but is no longer task-agnostic (contradicts part 1 of the project). Rejected.

  - Proposed: matches L1 of the pipeline. Con: the 1-2 km labels are incomplete.

## 19 - Future work

Steps planned **after** the QCAI paper (tag **[future]**, not in the schedule of section 2). They keep the first part of the pipeline unchanged (frozen backbones, self-supervised fusion F-X, cached fused maps, stage 1, QK training samples and test candidates of QH4-QH5) and change only the task-specific quantum branch, so that every new method is compared with the QSVC of QI4 on **the same inputs, the same simulator (Qiskit Aer statevector, same node) and the same metrics**. The variational methods (VQC, VQR) and QAOA were left out of the paper because the literature favours kernel methods for small-data classification (convex training, no barren plateaus; see the Introduction of section 15); they are added here because the PhD research proposal asks for them explicitly, and because QI4.4 showed that the QSVC alone is not enough (QD11). Like the rest of the experiment, these are simulator studies: indicative comparisons, no claim of quantum advantage.

### FW1 - Stage 2 with the information of stage 1 (QD11 option B)
- [ ] **FW1.1** [future] **Stage-1 score as input**: the stage-1 logit added to the PCA components (one extra feature, one extra qubit or a classical term), or stage 2 as a re-ranker of the stage-1 score (final score = calibrated combination of the stage-1 logit and the stage-2 margin, weights fitted in the spatial CV of QI4.2). Metric: the QI4.4 comparison at equal recall (does the combination beat stage 1 alone?).
- [ ] **FW1.2** [future] **Neighbourhood context**: the vectors of the 3 x 3 neighbouring sub-tiles (what stage 1 sees) pooled or concatenated before the reduction.
- [ ] **FW1.3** [future] **Supervised reduction** in place of PCA (QD6 option not adopted): e.g. the penultimate layer of the stage-1 head, or a projection fitted on S1-train labels (PLS, partial least squares / LDA, linear discriminant analysis), so that the d components keep the crater information that PCA loses (QG3: F-X 0.574 → 0.444 AP at d = 16).
  - Discussed with the user (2026-11-24), kept here (not in the paper): the limit of PCA is that it is unsupervised (it keeps the largest variance, not the crater information), not that it is linear; a 16-d bottleneck inside the self-supervised fusion, or an unsupervised MLP / autoencoder, would have the same limit (as the kernel PCA rejected in QD6). QJ2 (2026-11-24) then measured that at the stage-2 sample sizes (n ≤ 500) the PCA costs ~0 AP for F-X and the main loss is the number of training samples (0.07-0.12 AP). ### FW2 - Variational quantum classifier (VQC) for stage 2
- Note (2026-11-24, with the user): VQC / VQR stay out of the paper (with the same encoding a variational model is a restricted linear model in the feature space of the kernel, Schuld 2021, so it is not expected to beat the QSVC on these data); in the **full project** they should be implemented from the start next to the kernels, for scalability (training on mini-batches, one circuit per candidate at inference, against a kernel cost quadratic in the data), with the barren-plateau diagnostics of FW2.3 and the classical baselines of QJ1.

### FW6 - Trainable quantum kernel (quantum kernel alignment)
**Idea.** The kernels of QI2 have one free parameter, the bandwidth c, chosen on a grid of 5 values. A trainable kernel puts many free parameters θ in the encoding circuit U(x; θ), optimises them on the training data so that the kernel separates the classes, then **freezes** them: the SVM (QSVC, or QSVR for QI7) is trained on the frozen kernel exactly as in QI4 and inference is unchanged (it is not a kernel that adapts during inference). It sits between the QSVC (fixed circuit, convex training) and the VQC of FW2 (circuit trained by gradient): the circuit is trained like a VQC, the final model keeps the guaranteed SVM training. Question answered: does any circuit of a whole family beat the fixed maps of QI2 and the RBF of QJ1? (QI5.3: g ≈ 1 for the fixed maps.) References: Hubregtsen et al. 2022 (training quantum embedding kernels), Glick et al. 2024 (covariant quantum kernels); Qiskit Machine Learning `TrainableFidelityQuantumKernel` + `QuantumKernelTrainer`.
- [ ] **FW6.1** [future] **Model**: kernel K_θ(x, x') = |⟨0| U(x'; θ)† U(x; θ) |0⟩|² (fidelity, as QK-A / QK-Z) and the projected version on the single-qubit expectations of U(x; θ) (as QK-P). U(x; θ) = L ∈ {1, 2, 3} repeated layers (data re-uploading) of: trainable single-qubit rotations, the data encoded as in QK-A / QK-Z with a **trainable scale per component** (angle θ_i · x_i: the per-qubit generalisation of c), a linear chain of entangling gates. Same inputs as QI4 (d standardised PCA components, d qubits); ~3 L d parameters. **Initialisation** at the CV-chosen fixed map of QI4.3 (scales = c), so training can only start from the best fixed kernel.
- [ ] **FW6.2** [future] **Training of θ**: maximise the **centred kernel-target alignment** of QI5.2 (it ranked the encodings like the CV) on mini-batches of the training fold (B ≈ 64-128 samples: B² kernel values per step), Adam, gradients by automatic differentiation of the statevector on the simulator (by the parameter-shift rule on hardware); early stopping on the alignment of the validation fold. Then, θ frozen, the C of the SVM chosen by the spatial CV of QI4.2 (θ trained on the training folds only, never on the validation fold or the test).
- [ ] **FW6.3** [future] **Comparison** with the same protocol, samples, test candidates and metrics as QI4.3-QI4.4: against the fixed maps (QI4.3), the RBF and linear SVM (QJ1), and, for fairness, a **classical trainable kernel** trained by the same alignment objective (RBF with one length scale per component, "ARD", automatic relevance determination).
- [ ] **FW6.4** [future] **Diagnostics**: alignment and CV AP during training; concentration (QI5.1) and geometric difference against the RBF (QI5.3) of the trained kernel (does training raise g above ~1?); variance of the gradients vs d and L (barren plateaus); circuit evaluations and time.
- [ ] **FW6.5** [future] **Reading**: if the trained kernel equals the RBF (same AP, g ≈ 1), "a classical kernel suffices" holds for a whole family of circuits, not only for three fixed ones; a gain would point to the parameters that matter (which scales, layers).
